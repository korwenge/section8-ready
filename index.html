Part 3 — Ground-specific readiness

Tick the ones where you'd have the evidence today if you needed that ground. The four grounds below cover most real possession cases.

Ground 1ASelling the property4 months' notice · mandatory

Not usable in the first 12 months of the tenancy. After using it you cannot re-let or market the property for 12 months — misuse carries serious penalties. Evidence to hold: estate agent instruction, solicitor engagement letter, marketing records.

Ground 1You or close family moving in4 months' notice · mandatory

Same 12-month protected period and re-letting ban. Covers parent, grandparent, sibling, child, grandchild. Evidence to hold: a sworn statement of intent, plus anything showing the genuine plan (e.g. family member's notice on their current home).

Ground 8Serious rent arrears4 weeks' notice · mandatory

Threshold is now 3 months' arrears (13 weeks if paid weekly/fortnightly) — and it must hold both when you serve notice and at the hearing. Universal Credit housing-element delays don't count toward the total. Evidence to hold: the rent ledger, bank statements, the arrears letter trail. Pair with discretionary Grounds 10/11 as backup.

Grounds 12 / 14 / 7ABreach & anti-social behaviourvaries · 7A immediate

Mostly discretionary — the judge weighs your whole file. Evidence to hold: dated incident log, neighbour complaints in writing, police reference numbers, photos, and your written warnings to the tenant.

Part 4 — Council-inspection readiness

Local authorities can now demand documents and inspect with less warning. The same file that wins in court satisfies an inspector.
